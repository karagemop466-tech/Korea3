Why the answer is "none" and why that is the honest answer. Eighteen rules were written down before they were tested, and each was tested once. A rule that passes an uncorrected 5% test out of eighteen is what chance produces: at 18 independent tests the expected number of false passes is 0.9. The Bonferroni bound is applied so the chance of any false pass across the family stays at 5%. Nothing clears it, and the smallest uncorrected p-value is far above the per-test threshold. Reporting a "best rule" from this table would be reporting noise with a source link attached.
