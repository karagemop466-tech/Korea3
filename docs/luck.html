Luck vs Skill

Zero-skill simulation bands, edge monotonicity and the controls that keep the analysis honest.

Raw ROI is a terrible scoreboard: with a few hundred bets, luck swings results by more than any realistic edge. Three separate tools are used to strip it out.

1 · Zero-skill simulation

For every bet the outcome is re-simulated using the de-vigged market probability of the side we took — a bettor with zero skill making exactly the same bets. 10,000 draws give the ROI distribution that pure luck produces at that sample size. The p-value is how often luck matches or beats the observed ROI. Source: validate.py.

Controls that keep us honest

  • Always-home ATS: -4.88% — the vig, minus a small home-cover shortfall.
  • Always-favourite ATS: 0.21% — near zero, as an efficient market should be.
  • Always-over: -3.41% — the under side of an inflated total.
  • Coin-flip ATS: -3.53% — pure vig, the cost of playing at random.
Known bias in the null

The always-favourite control shows p = 0.0156 for a 0.21% ROI. That is a false positive created by the de-vig method: proportional de-vigging systematically understates favourites' true probabilities, so the zero-skill null is biased low for favourite sides. It is disclosed as IR-04 and is the reason p-values for moneyline strategies must be read with care.
