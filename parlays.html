Parlays and same-game combos

Single bets, cross-game parlays and same-game combinations, priced with measured dependence rather than assumed independence.

The independence assumption is the whole product. A parlay price is a product of leg probabilities. If the legs are positively dependent, the book's multiplicative price is too generous; if they are negatively dependent, it is too stingy. The tables below measure which case you are in.

Why cross-game parlays are still bad value

Cross-game legs are effectively independent (phi 0.006), so the price is arithmetically fair. That does not make it profitable: the bookmaker's hold compounds with every leg. At the measured average hold of 2.72% per market, a two-leg card carries roughly 5.52% of embedded margin before any skill, and the S9 parlay lost -0.47% per card over 338 cards. hold calculation ↗
