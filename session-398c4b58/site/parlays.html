Parlays & Same-Game

Independence tests, correlation-adjusted pricing, and parlay performance.

A parlay multiplies prices, which is only correct if the legs are independent. We tested that assumption instead of assuming it.

Cross-game independence

Correlation between the ATS outcome of one game and a randomly paired game in the same week: -0.0032 (95% CI -0.0290 … 0.0218, n = 5,703). The interval contains zero, so multiplying cross-game leg prices is sound. Source: correlations.py.csv · correlations.py.

Finding

Same-game correlations are tiny: the empirical joint probability is within about ±2% of the product of the two leg rates, consistent with the measured r of 0.020. The popular idea that a book which multiplies spread and total prices is exploitable does not survive the data.

P01/P02 legs come from different games and are priced from observed leg prices, so their ROI is real. P03, T08 and T09 are priced with the independence assumption and are therefore price-unverified. T09 (same player, targets + receptions) shows a spectacular ROI precisely because those two legs correlate at r = 0.92 — a naive multiplier would be badly wrong, which is why real books do not price it that way.

Why sample size matters

The 3-leg parlay returns +13.5% over 370 bets, but its 95% confidence interval runs from −13.7% to +41.4%. With a hit rate of 15.4% on a 3-leg parlay, a 370-bet sample cannot distinguish a real edge from noise.
