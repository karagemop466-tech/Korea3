### A02 — Different correlation samples reconcile; one publication label remains defective

The “Home cover x Over” row in [`data/derived/corr_same_game.csv`](../data/derived/corr_same_game.csv) reports all-season `n=6784`, `phi=0.0086`, 95% interval `[-0.0162, +0.0332]`, and `p=0.4783`. The [recomputation record](../hub/data/recompute.json) also documents a 2006–2025 window. I independently recalculated the binary phi from [`data/derived/model_dataset.csv`](../data/derived/model_dataset.csv): all seasons give **n = 6,784, phi = +0.008609**; 2006–2025 gives **n = 4,996, phi = +0.016283**. In that pipeline, the pair is formed from nonmissing `home_cover` and `over` indicators, which excludes pushes; it does not require recorded odds for both legs or a non-tied moneyline outcome ([dataset builder](../scripts/build_dataset.py), [correlation calculation](../scripts/correlation.py)). No confidence interval or p-value for this 2006–2025 recomputation is asserted here.

The separate root [`correlations.html`](../correlations.html) reports **n = 4,853, phi = +0.016109**. This is reproducible from the committed [`line_lab/data/derived/features.csv`](../line_lab/data/derived/features.csv), [`line_lab/correlations.py`](../line_lab/correlations.py) and [`line_lab/data/derived/summary.json`](../line_lab/data/derived/summary.json): `same_game_dependence` first requires nonmissing `spread_line`, `total_line`, `home_spread_odds` and `over_odds`, then drops pushes in ATS/total and home-score ties because it computes all three pairs on one common sample (including the home-moneyline pair). The two 2006–2025 files contain the same 5,199 unique game IDs and matching scores, lines and relevant price fields. Within the 4,996 rows with settled cover/over indicators, **130** lack one or both required odds fields; a further **13** odds-complete rows are home-score ties excluded from the line-lab's shared three-pair sample. Thus `4,996 − 130 − 13 = 4,853`. This resolves the apparent 4,853-vs-4,996 count discrepancy: they are reproducible estimates on different eligibility rules, not one sample with inconsistent arithmetic.

A separate reporting defect remains: the all-season `n=6,784` row in `corr_same_game.csv` is associated with prose describing 2006–2025. Existing [flag X02](../hub/flagged.html#X02) records that mismatch; this audit did not edit the canonical data or dashboard. **Proposed, not approved:** label each estimate with its precise period, odds requirements, and push/tie handling, and keep the line-lab and hub/pipeline ledgers distinct.

The [NIST correlation reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/correlat.htm) defines correlation as a measure of linear association; NIST's [correlation-confidence-limits reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/corrconf.htm) describes a Fisher-normal-approximation interval for a correlation coefficient. These references support statistical interpretation, not this dataset's contents. The checked-in [`hub/check_decisions.py`](../hub/check_decisions.py) verifies the separate two-leg −110 break-even arithmetic reference **phi = 0.0975**. The coefficients are local computations on third-party data, not official NFL statistics, and do not validate any sportsbook's same-game pricing or establish a betting signal.

### A05 — Positive backtest results remain insufficient evidence of skill

The checked-in [gate ledger](../data/derived/gates_results.csv) contains 18 strategy/rule rows and every row has `verdict=FAIL`; four rows are labelled `PROVISIONAL` as a tier but still fail the verdict. S14 (“Wind 15+ mph: unders”) is a useful illustration of why raw profit and skill must be separated: the repository ledger reports n=607, ROI=+8.41%, a nominal 95% interval of [+0.75%, +16.07%], p=0.0160, and multiplicity-adjusted q=0.2879. It fails G4, whose published definition requires q ≤ 0.10 for the tested family; 29.97% of its prices are imputed, and its source field says the wind variable is unverified and has no source field. These are repository-derived historical calculations, not official NFL statistics or an independently verified edge.

The [NIST multiple-comparison guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section4/prc47.htm) cautions that repeating pairwise tests does not preserve a single-comparison significance level. A favorable in-sample estimate—especially after trying multiple candidate rules—does not by itself establish prospective skill. The [competition/reward page](../hub/competition.html) keeps the promotion gates and reward amounts unverified/unfunded; no funded prize or verified winning signal is claimed here.

**Proposed, not approved:** keep candidate discovery separate from a frozen confirmation protocol; publish the full tested family and multiplicity method; require time-stamped, executable prices and a genuinely forward sample; report uncertainty and price-imputation rates; and prohibit reward eligibility until all pre-registered gates pass. No money, betting account, or execution path is involved in this audit.

`verify/run_all.sh` calls [`verify/source_audit.py`](../verify/source_audit.py) with `--allow-dirs session-398c4b58,qa`. The pass is accurate for the configured budget, but should not be summarized as “zero unsourced rows on the site.” The report's 46 is a local source-audit count, not an official NFL statistic. A future owner should triage each row, blank unsupported factual values and retain an explicit exception register rather than relying indefinitely on broad directory exemptions.

The source classifier also does not list member-club domains among its official hosts. As a result, an official team page can be classified as an unclassified external/dataset link even when it is an authentic club publisher. That is the unresolved source-tier question Q15/S13, not proof that the club page itself is unofficial.

7. **Official data access:** use manual review or a licensed/authorized feed where necessary; do not automate systematic retrieval from NFL.com without permission under its published terms.
