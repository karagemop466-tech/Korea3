So any correlation whose definition needs a market number is left blank with a reason, rather than computed from a third-party file and still called a correlation.

The one number that should change how a card is priced. The same-game card's two legs win together 24.63% of the time, against 26.01% if they were independent. A card priced at the product of its legs is therefore worse for the bettor than its price suggests, and the gap is the measured phi of -0.0551 over 2,521 cards.

The formula the missing input would feed

For a two-leg card at decimal prices d1 and d2 with marginal win probabilities p1 and p2, the card breaks even when the joint probability is 1 / (d1 x d2). The dependence the card needs is therefore

phi_required = ( 1/(d1 x d2) - p1 x p2 ) / sqrt( p1(1-p1) x p2(1-p2) )

Every term except the joint is a price. No official publisher prints those prices, so this page publishes the formula and leaves the answer blank. Give it prices and it fills in one line.
