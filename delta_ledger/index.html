What changed since the last session looked

The gate that was open is now partly cleared. Every readiness row about the capture was written against an empty manifest ("0 of 33 per day recorded"). The manifest now holds 132 rows over 4 scheduled runs on 3 distinct days, so the "two or more timestamped reads" precondition is met for 33 of 33 sources. That is a number, not an intention, and it is the first one of its kind in this repository.

The mechanism it clears is useless as built. The only change detector the capture has is a SHA-256 over the raw body. 99 of 99 transitions were labelled "changed" and 96 of them had an identical table and row count, a 97.0% false-positive rate. "Publish the delta, never the level" and "make the injury signal a surprise, not a level" cannot be satisfied by that signal: a page with a nonce or a timestamp in it looks brand new every four hours. The structural signature recorded at capture time (table and row counts) is the detector that works, and it is what this desk pins bands on.

The alarm the brief asked for did not exist, and it would have fired. Between the runs of 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19 the injury page's row count moved 28 against a median of 326 and back; the earlier value was 92% below the median and was recorded as result=ok with the marker present, so the byte-level check passed it. Two drift events are now named on the drift page, and check_drift.py turns them red.

What is still not true. No value is parsed anywhere: a value-level delta -- "Joseph went from Questionable to Out between Wednesday and Friday" -- is still 0 of what it needs, and it will stay 0 until the owner decides the terms question (readiness B33), because parsing is exactly what the private/public split is about. This desk publishes the *shape* of the pages, which is a fact about our capture, not about any club's roster.
