Origination order: side → total → moneyline

The brief fixes the origination order: originate the side first, then the total, then the moneyline. This page records that order as policy, shows the three steps side by side for the 2026 book, and closes the gap this review found: the published book originated the side and the total but no own-moneyline price. The fair moneyline is now derived here, as a labelled recomputation, from the model win probabilities the repo already publishes — no new model, no market input.

Policy

the order, and what each step may use

  • Side first. The spread is originated from expected points (home − away); the model publishes it for every game. Evidence: summary.json live rows ↗.
  • Total second. The total is originated from the same expected points (home + away) — simultaneous in the arithmetic, second in the ordering policy.
  • Moneyline third. The fair price is a conversion of the win probability the same model publishes (this lab, build.py ↗): it adds no new model and takes no market input, so the book stays independent of the market it is later compared against.
  • Then, and only then, comparison. Fair lines are compared with market numbers (separate columns, never averaged) and any difference becomes a candidate signal through the pre-registered rules — not a line change.

Definitions used above

why the moneyline difference is a probability, not points

Fair moneyline
American price with zero vig implied by the model win probability p: decimal 1/p converted to American (negative below even money, positive above). Pure arithmetic; labelled recomputed.
Diff (moneyline)
Fair home win probability minus the de-vigged market home win probability, in percentage points, published only where a two-sided market moneyline exists. Prices are not signed lines, so no point difference is defined — the probability difference is.
Diff (spread / total)
Fair minus market, the same convention as the published agreement columns; equality with those is re-checked by the verifier on every build.
