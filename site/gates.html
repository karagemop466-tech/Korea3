Discipline

Validation gates

Eight mandatory gates. A strategy is promoted only if it clears all eight. Any gate that cannot be evaluated for lack of a source is reported as "n/a" — never as a pass.

Why gates at all

A backtest is an experiment, and the more experiments you run the more winners you will find by accident. This family runs 18 rules over 7,075 games. At a naive 5% threshold you would expect about one "discovery" even if every rule were worthless. The gates exist to make that failure mode expensive.

Removing luck: the four methods used

  1. Exact binomial test against break-even. Break-even at −110 is 52.381%. Each rule's win count is tested one-sided against that, not against 50%.
  2. False-discovery-rate control. Benjamini–Hochberg across the whole family. In this run 0 of 18 rules were rejected at q ≤ 0.1 — the strongest possible statement that none of the apparent edges survives a multiplicity correction.
  3. Coin-flip Monte Carlo. For each rule, 20,000 simulated bettors with break-even skill placed the same number of bets at the same prices. The reported coinflip_p is the share of simulations that matched or beat the observed profit. It answers the lay question directly: "how often does a coin flip do this?"
  4. Empirical-Bayes shrinkage. Across the family, the observed dispersion of edges is compared with the sampling noise. The prior variance of true edges comes out at τ² = 0.000636, so the shrink factor is τ²/(τ² + SE²) per rule. Mean observed edge -1.20% shrinks to -0.82% — 32% of the measured edge in this family is attributable to luck.
Result: 0 of 18 rules passed all eight gates. Four are PROVISIONAL — they fail G4 only, the multiplicity gate, and need more sample, not a different model.

What "provisional" means, precisely

Provisional is not a soft pass. It means: the rule is positive in both windows, survives a 5-cent price stress, survives a change of line source, and still has a positive edge after shrinkage — but the record is not long enough to survive being one of eighteen rules. Practically, it is a rule worth tracking, not a rule worth staking. The required additional bets for each are computed on Single Bets.
