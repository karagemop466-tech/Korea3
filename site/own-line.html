Origination

Our own line

A spread and a total produced from team ratings only, week by week, with no market input. Then benchmarked honestly against the closing line — including when that makes us look bad.

The model, stated completely

For every game we model each team's points:

points = μ + offence[team] + defence[opponent] + hfa·(home) + β·situational

  • Own spread = predicted home points − predicted away points, flipped into the conventional home-spread orientation; own total = the sum.
  • No leakage: for each (season, week) the model is fitted only on games whose kickoff date is strictly earlier than the first game of that week.
  • Recency weighting: exponential decay, half-life 365 days, three seasons of history.
  • Ridge penalty: λ = 4.0 on team ratings, fixed a priori and never tuned on the evaluation window.
  • Neutral-site games (international and Super Bowl) carry no home-field term.

Conclusion: the own line is a legitimate independent line and it is strictly dominated by the close. Its information content, where it exists at all, is too small to improve a forecast. Any strategy built purely on "my line differs from the market" is a strategy for donating vig — which is exactly what rule S01 measures (-7.0% over 2,642 bets).
