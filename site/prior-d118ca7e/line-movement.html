Line movement, closing-line value and the price of a point

Why this page exists

A backtest can be earned by luck. Closing-line value cannot: the close is the market's final, money-weighted opinion, so consistently taking a better number than the close is the closest thing to a direct measurement of skill. This page reports what the data can actually support — the size of the line move, whether our own line anticipates it, and what one point of a better number is worth in win probability.

The uncomfortable result first. Betting our model's side against the earlier line produces a mean closing-line value of about 0.10 points and a full-window ROI of -1.8%. The model is not sharp enough to capture the move it disagrees with, so there is no closing-line-value story to tell here (F-05).

What one point of a better number is worth — measured, not assumed

Every game is re-priced one point better for the home side and the ATS result is recomputed (pushes counted as half a win). The difference in win share is the empirical value of a point.

Reading: at the standard price of −110 a point has to be worth more than about 2.4 percentage points to cover the vig, and it is worth roughly 4.3 — comfortably more. That is the whole economic argument for line shopping, and the reason closing-line value is worth measuring at all.

Does our line anticipate the move?

Model edge versus the earlier line, sorted into quintiles, against the move that actually followed. Correlation: 0.0106 on 2,043 games; the model's direction matched the market's on 51.0% of games where both were non-zero. That is a coin flip. A positive correlation means the market tends to move toward our line. Even a small positive value here is evidence of information, not of profit: a book's margin still has to be beaten.

A strategy can show positive ROI at the earlier line and still be a loser once the number is gone; the CLV column is what separates the two. A positive ROI with negative CLV means the profit came from results, not from information.

Closing-line value is zero by construction in the main bet stream. Bets are priced at the dataset closing line, so CLV against that same close is 0.000 for every bet by construction. Any report of non-zero CLV from this bet stream would be a bug. The early-line strategies above are the analysis that can actually measure CLV. (12,936 ATS bets, 12,936 of them exactly at the close.)
