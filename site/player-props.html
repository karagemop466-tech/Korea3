Player-prop combos

The framework is complete. The numbers are not — and they are deliberately blank. There is no official, verifiable player-prop line source available to this project, so no correlation value is claimed.

Read this before the table. The NFL does not publish prop prices, and the pinned open dataset contains no player-level box scores. Every measured prop correlation on this page would therefore be invented. They are left blank and listed on Flagged for Review as open task B01. What is published: the mechanism that fixes the sign of each relationship, the exact correlation each combo must clear to be worth its price, and the settlement rules with primary-source links.

Two consequences that do not depend on any measurement:

  • A positive correlation only helps if the book failed to price it. Operators explicitly forbid correlated legs outside their own Same Game Parlay product, and re-price the ones they do offer. FanDuel's house rules: parlay legs whose outcomes affect one another are "not permitted, unless explicitly offered by FanDuel Sportsbook, e.g., as a Same Game Parlay bet". DraftKings reserves the right to void correlated legs entirely.
  • A negative correlation helps you even at an unadjusted price — because the joint probability falls below the product, which the posted decimal does not reflect. That is why P02, P03 and P06 (the negative-sign combos) are the only structurally interesting ones.

Settlement rules that change the maths

  • Must play at least one snap. DraftKings: "On any player prop market, player(s) must play at least one snap for bets to have action." A voided leg does not simply drop out of a same-game parlay — the ticket is re-priced at bet-placement odds. That is a real, asymmetric risk in a combo.
  • Re-pricing, not voiding. For pre-live same-game parlays, DraftKings re-prices the ticket using the odds available at the time of placement rather than reducing the leg count.
  • Overtime counts. FanDuel settles on the result declared by the governing body "including any overtime played" — which matters for any prop with a clock or a drive-count dependency.

What can be done without a prop feed

  1. Use the measured spread × total φ as a prior. Game-level outcomes that feel correlated (scoring vs covering) measured +0.0086. Assume player-level pairs, which have less in common, are not larger by default. Demand evidence.
  2. Only negative-sign combos are structurally worth researching, because they are the ones an unadjusted price helps.
  3. Log the posted price. Without it, no prop research is auditable — which is exactly the state this project is in, and exactly why the row is flagged rather than filled.
