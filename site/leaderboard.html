Competition

Leaderboard

A leaderboard that pays for verified edge. The design problem is not ranking winners — it is preventing luck from getting ranked at all.

The problem this competition solves

Run enough backtests and something always looks good. In this project 18 rules were tested and the best raw result is +8.4% over 607 bets — and it still does not clear the multiplicity bar. A naive leaderboard would have crowned it. A useful one must first subtract the part of that return that is explained by variance, and it must charge strategies for how many of them are competing.

Scoring

Score = 60% luck-adjusted return + 20% confirmation return + 10% robustness + 10% coverageSource: scripts/gates.py; design in research/leaderboard.md
WeightComponentDefinitionShareWhat it rewards
1Luck-adjusted return (primary)Empirical-Bayes shrunk ROI: τ²/(τ²+SE²) × observed ROI60%Rewards edge that survives its own sample sizedesign
2Out-of-sample returnROI in the confirmation window only (2016–2026)20%Rewards rules that still work after discoverydesign
3RobustnessGates G5 + G6 + G7 pass10%Rewards edges that are not one season or one bookdesign
4CoverageSettled bets, capped at 1,00010%Rewards evidence volume without letting volume win outrightdesign

The primary component is deliberately not raw ROI. Raw ROI is the number that flatters small samples; the shrunk estimate is the one that does not. Two strategies with the same raw ROI are ranked by sample size, and the one with fewer bets loses.

Anti-gaming rules

  1. Pre-registration. A rule must be written down before its evaluation window is run. This repo's 18 rules are in scripts/signals.py; adding a rule after seeing results taints the family and the family size increases the FDR penalty for everyone.
  2. No dropping losers. Every rule that is tested is published. A tournament where you can delete your losses measures nothing.
  3. Fixed snapshot cadence. Evaluation happens at declared snapshots, not "when it looks good". Repeated looks at a live record is the same sin as many rules at one look.
  4. Price honesty. Only the recorded price counts. Line-shopping masquerades as skill unless the price is fixed.
  5. No post-hoc sample trimming. No starting the record in a convenient season, no excluding "weird" weeks without declaring it in advance.
  6. Audit trail. Each submission carries its data hash, its code, and the exact window. Anything not reproducible is ineligible.

Reward design

Rewards should scale with the tier, never with the raw return, or the board teaches contestants to chase variance. Concretely: only Verified and Defended strategies are eligible for any payout at all, and the payout pool is split evenly across them rather than weighted by ROI — because weighting by ROI reintroduces exactly the bias the gates exist to remove. A strategy that fails a single gate in a later snapshot drops a tier immediately; there is no grace period, because the point of the exercise is that survival is fragile.
