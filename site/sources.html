Sources

Every claim on this site maps to one of these sources. Tiers are disclosed because they are not equal: official league sources govern rules, licensed operators govern settlement, and an open dataset is the only place where machine-readable NFL market numbers exist.

Tier definitions

  • Tier 1 — Official NFL. Rules, scoring, timing, overtime, tie handling, and the league's own explanation of how spread pricing works. Authoritative for mechanics.
  • Tier 2 — Licensed operator. The actual counterparty for a bet. Authoritative for what happens to a stake: void rules, re-pricing, correlated-leg policy, minimum participation.
  • Tier 3 — Open dataset. The only machine-readable source of NFL lines available here. Not official, fully disclosed, pinned by commit and checksummed so a reader can verify any row.

What has no official source

This is the important part. The following cannot be confirmed from any official publication, and are therefore not published on this site:

  • Player-prop prices and prop correlation values. No official or machine-readable source. Blank, flagged as B01.
  • Historical closing lines "as published by the books". Books do not publish archives; the open dataset is the only available record, and it is Tier 3.
  • Injury reports and inactives. Published weekly as PDFs but not machine-readable here; not used. Flagged as B02.
  • Weather ground truth. The dataset carries temperature and wind with no source field; labelled UNVERIFIED wherever shown. Flagged as B03.
  • Venue coordinates and travel distances. No verified source in the dataset, so no travel signal was tested. Flagged as B05.

Source anomalies found during verification

Two NFL Support articles have their bodies swapped. The page titled "What is a money line?" explains the point spread, and the page titled "What is the point spread?" explains the moneyline. The content is correct in both cases; the titles are crossed. Both are cited above, and both are listed on Flagged for Review. The −110 statement and the push rule used throughout this site come from those two pages, which is why the anomaly matters: a reader following the link will land on a page whose title does not match its body.
