Parlays

A parlay multiplies the payout. It also multiplies whatever advantage or disadvantage the legs already have. The only thing that can rescue a parlay is correlation — so this page measures correlation rather than assuming it.

The arithmetic that decides everything

Two legs at −110 pay a decimal of (210/110)² = 3.6446. If the legs are independent coin flips, the joint probability is 25%, so the expected return per unit is 0.25 × 3.6446 − 1 = −8.9% — double the −4.5% you pay on a single. The parlay did not add edge; it doubled the tax.

The joint probability of two outcomes with marginals p₁, p₂ and correlation φ is exactly:

P(both) = p₁p₂ + φ·√(p₁(1−p₁)p₂(1−p₂))

Setting that against the price gives the break-even correlation: φ ≥ 0.0975 for two legs at −110 when each leg is a true coin flip, and 0.0749 for three legs.

The popular parlay is the worst one. Spread × total is the most-played same-game combination and its measured φ is +0.0086 (95% CI -0.0162 to +0.0332, p = 0.48). That is indistinguishable from zero, and it sits an order of magnitude below the 0.0975 needed to break even.
Moneyline × spread on the same team is the one pair that clears the bar — and it is already priced. φ = 0.672, with the joint probability of both legs landing at 43.9% against 27.2% if independent — a 16.7 percentage-point gap. Every licensed operator either re-prices this pair or refuses it: FanDuel's house rules state that parlay legs whose outcomes affect one another are not permitted "unless explicitly offered ... as a Same Game Parlay"; DraftKings reserves the right to void correlated legs. See Sources.

Across games: independence holds

If different games were correlated, a cross-game parlay could be built profitably. Testing 200 random game pairs in each of 28 seasons gives a pooled mean φ of -0.0239 (SE 0.0146, p = 0.10). Independence is not rejected — so there is no cross-game correlation to exploit.

The honest reading: strategies that bet different markets on the same games are close to independent, which means combining them in a parlay buys no correlation advantage and simply multiplies the tax. The strongly correlated pairs are structural — the same market, opposite sides — and are labelled as such rather than presented as findings.

Verdict

No positive-EV parlay construction was found in this dataset. The threshold is now explicit: a leg pair must clear φ ≈ 0.098 at −110. Exactly one NFL relationship does, and the market already charges for it. Any parlay strategy claiming otherwise must publish its measured φ through the same gates as the single bets — which is what the leaderboard requires.
