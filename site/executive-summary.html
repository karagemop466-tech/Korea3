Executive Summary

Executive summary

One page, no marketing. What was built, what it found, and what it refuses to claim. Generated 2026-11-01 from the pinned dataset.

1. Scope and what "originating a line" means here

We build a spread and a total from team ratings only, week by week, using nothing that was published after the week's first kickoff. That is the only honest definition of an own line: a number that exists before the market's number is allowed to influence it. The model is deliberately simple and fully specified: points scored = league mean + offence rating + opponent defence rating + home-field term, ridge-penalised, recency-weighted. It is fitted 4 ways (ratings only, + rest, + division, + dome) and re-run across nine hyper-parameter settings, so the published number is not a cherry-picked specification.

The paired test on absolute error is decisive: our line loses to the closing line by 0.423 points per game (p = 4.9e-24) and on totals by 0.266 (p = 1.3e-14). Betting our disagreement with the market loses money — the 3-point-edge rule returns -7.0% and the 5-point version -6.5%.

There is one nuance worth keeping: in a regression of the actual margin on both lines, our line does carry a small amount of information the close does not (coefficient +0.118, p = 0.011). It is real but too small to use: a blend weighted 10.9% on our line and fitted on 2000–2015 fails to improve out-of-sample accuracy (+0.0084 pts, p = 0.30). For totals there is no information at all (p = 0.44) and the fitted blend weight is zero.

3. Eighteen rules, eight gates, zero promotions

Every rule was written down before its window was evaluated, and every rule is published — including the ones that look bad, and there are 18 of those. The gates are deliberately harsh because the base rate of false discovery in this kind of search is enormous: with 18 independent tests at the 5% level you expect roughly one spurious "winner" even if nothing works.

Luck-adjusted ROI is the empirical-Bayes posterior mean after shrinking toward zero by the amount of noise in each estimate (family prior variance τ² = 0.000636). It is the number the leaderboard ranks on, because it is the only one that penalises a small sample.

4. The single most interesting measured quantity

Same-game correlation is far smaller than punters assume. Over 6784 games, home-cover and over are correlated at φ = +0.0086 (95% CI -0.0162 to +0.0332) — indistinguishable from zero. Yet a two-leg parlay priced as if independent at −110 needs φ ≥ 0.0975 to break even when each leg is a true coin flip. The gap between 0.009 and 0.098 is the entire reason parlay volume is profitable for the house.

5. What we will and will not claim

  • Claimed: the pipeline, the model, the backtests, the gates, the correlations. All of it reproduces from the pinned data with the scripts in this repo.
  • Claimed: no rule in this family has demonstrated skill at the 10% false-discovery level. Four have demonstrated survival, which is not the same thing.
  • Not claimed: any player-prop correlation value. There is no verifiable prop-line source available here, so the numbers are blank and the items are flagged. See Player Props.
  • Not claimed: that a signal which failed here cannot work with better data. It means this rule, on this data, does not clear this bar.

6. What a serious next session should do

  1. Extend the sample in the four provisional rules with live, timestamped closing lines (their blocker is purely sample size — the required bet counts are on Single Bets).
  2. Add player-level data and a licensed prop feed so prop correlations can be measured instead of reasoned about.
  3. Add injury/inactive reporting: the single largest known omission from the feature set.
  4. Re-run the gates monthly as a fixed-horizon test so that multiplicity stays controlled over time, not just within one snapshot.
