Method and limits

How the capture, the parse and the join were done, and what they cannot tell you.

How this was made

6 steps
#StepWhat it means
1Claim the scopeA block was appended to COORDINATION.md naming the session, the file scope and the tasks, and pushed before any scoped file was touched.
2Read the pagesEach club's own /team/depth-chart page and the NFL's /injuries/ page were opened in full, chunk by chunk, until the tables were exhausted.
3Transcribe verbatimTable rows were written into data/raw/*.json exactly as printed, including empty cells, co-listed cells, wrapped continuation rows and club-specific position labels.
4Parse without inventingdepth_lab/parse_depth.py turns layout into rows. It trims trailing padding columns, keeps interior blanks, numbers co-listed players at the same string, and never fills a cell.
5Join under a printed ruledepth_lab/join_replacements.py applies the rule printed at the top of the replacements page, and writes an audit of every name it could not match.
6Build and fail closeddepth_lab/build.py refuses to emit a row without a source link; depth_lab/check.py fails the build if any page links a non-HTTPS or non-official host in a source column, or if a generated number disagrees with the data files.

Limits

Read these before using anything on this site.

8 limits
LimitWhat it means for a reader
The capture is a snapshot, not a live feedAll 31 club pages and the injury report were read once, on 2026-11-13. Clubs update charts on their own cadence and most print no date, so a chart captured today may already differ.
Club charts are the clubs' own opinion, by their own admissionNew England prints that the chart reflects the opinions of Patriots.com writers and not necessarily the organisation. No club in this capture presents its chart as an official competitive document except Arizona.
The club chart does not say who will playA depth chart is a publication, not a game-day designation. The NFL's own injury report is the official availability document, and even it prints an empty Game Status for many players.
Houston could not be readThe Texans page renders its tables with browser script. Rather than substitute a roster or a third-party site, Houston is left out of every depth table and logged on the flagged page.
Four clubs are absent from the injury reportChicago, Denver, Philadelphia and Tampa Bay had no injury table on the official page at capture time. The reason is not known and is not inferred.
Co-listings are unranked by definitionWhen a club prints two names in one cell, no order between them exists in the source. Any ranking would be invented.
No projection, price, rating or bet value appears anywhere in this buildThis build has no model in it. It publishes what official pages said and what they did not say.
Name matching is conservative on purposeThree hand-checked aliases reconcile a club's spelling with the league's. Two near-misses that a fuzzy matcher would have joined are deliberately left unjoined because they are different people.

Answering the question that prompted this build

An earlier session concluded, after probing four clubs and nfl.com, that ordered 2nd-, 3rd- and 4th-string depth was "not satisfiable from official sources". That conclusion was too broad. The correct, narrower finding is:

  • The league does not publish string order. nfl.com roster pages carry an ACT/RES status and no ranking, and the league's depth-chart path returns 404.
  • The member clubs do publish it. 31 of 32 club sites published an ordered chart that could be read, with columns for up to seven strings and names actually published out to 5th string: 928 players at 2nd string or deeper.
  • But the clubs disclaim it. Most print no date; New England states it is writers' opinion, not the organisation's; two clubs contradict themselves elsewhere on the same page.

So the data the brief asked for exists on official sites, and it is usable, provided every row is stamped with the club that published it and the blanks are left blank. That is what this build does.

Depth Lab is a capture and join of official publications. Club depth charts are the clubs' own pages; the injury report is the NFL's. Where an official page did not state a value, the cell is blank and flagged. No roster, news article, third-party site or model supplies a missing value.
