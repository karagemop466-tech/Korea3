Three weeks were opened here: 2026 W10, 2025 W1 and 2015 W10. All three returned that season’s report with the same table shape, so the archive is not a current-week snapshot. The rows for 2024 and 2009 are not published here, because nobody opened them in this session; the URL is printed so anyone can, and the claim stays unproven until they do. Backlog item B02 on the board asserted the report was “weekly PDFs only” — that is not what these three fetches show.

Three hazards on this page, each of which breaks an automated reader. (1) The page mutates under a fixed URL — H01. (2) The <title> can name a different week from the one the page shows — H02. (3) A week that has not been published yet redirects to the previous week rather than 404ing, so a weekly job asking for “this week” silently re-reads last week — I02.

Read that as a decision, not a complaint: the injury archive is deep enough to test injury rules, and the cost of using it is the number of page reads above. A session with outbound network access runs python3 -m injury_lab.capture fetch --season 2015 --week 10 per week; this sandbox cannot reach nfl.com at all, which is why the captures in this directory were read through the agent’s page fetcher and retained rather than re-fetched at will.

Refresh design, if the site is to stay true

  • Key the job on the fixture dates printed in the page, never on the week label or the title (H02).
  • Treat a redirect from the week you asked for as “not published yet”, and a 5xx on a club domain as “not captured” rather than “not published” (H10).
  • Re-hash the retained capture before trusting it, and keep the old one: history is the evidence, the live page is only an opinion with a date.
  • Publish the intra-week delta explicitly - the Wednesday report and the Saturday report are different inputs to the same fixture, and a model trained on one and bet on the other is a different strategy (see IG2 on the signals page).
