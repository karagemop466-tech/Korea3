Competition, rewards & luck

A competition design that pays for demonstrated skill, not for a lucky sample: pre-registration, closing-line value, calibration, lower confidence bounds, false-discovery control and a locked holdout. No prize is funded and no entry is open.

The competition in one paragraph

An entry is a fully specified strategy: market, selection rule, stake and settlement source. It is hashed before the evaluation window, tested once on a locked holdout, and ranked by a lower confidence bound on closing-line value with calibration and stability constraints. Only entries clearing all ten gates can appear in a paid tier. Because there is no timestamped quote archive, the board is empty.

Why a raw ROI leaderboard is not honest

A leaderboard that ranks raw return rewards variance. The mechanisms below each remove a specific way luck can masquerade as skill; their limits are stated so the design is not oversold.

These are statistical controls, not proof that a surviving entry is skilful.
Mechanism What it removes Its own limit Official source
Locked holdout Tuning to the evaluation set A single holdout can still be unlucky NIST-MULTI ↗
Lower confidence bound Small-sample luck Requires enough settled bets to be informative NIST-CI ↗
False-discovery control Mining many rules and reporting the best Power falls as the family grows NIST-BONF ↗
Permutation null A rule that looks good on this schedule only The null must be specified in advance NIST-BOOT ↗
Closing-line value Winning by taking stale or unavailable prices Needs a timestamped quote archive DK-MARKETS ↗
Calibration Confident but wrong probabilities A well-calibrated loser is still a loser REPO ↗
Minimum sample A hot streak of 30 bets Large samples take seasons to accumulate NIST-SAMPLESIZE ↗
Two-snapshot stability One lucky month Slower to promote a real edge NIST-MULTI ↗

Sample-size arithmetic

How many settled bets are needed to distinguish a true win rate from the 52.381% break-even at -110, with 80% power and a one-sided 5% test. Computed with the NIST formula; seasons assume one bet per game and 272 games per season. For parlay pricing, two true coin-flip legs at -110 need a phi coefficient of at least 0.0975 just to break even; that is an arithmetic identity, not a finding about any game.

Arithmetic under stated assumptions, not a forecast. Inputs: alpha 0.05, power 0.80, p0 = 110/210.

A 55% win rate — already a very large edge — needs roughly 2,242 bets, about eight seasons of one bet per game. This is why a 30-bet 'winner' is not evidence.
