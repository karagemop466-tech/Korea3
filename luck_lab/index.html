The removal-of-luck panel

What the numbers say. 10 of 11 strategies carry both a model probability and a closing-market probability on enough bets to be scored properly. None of them has a game-cluster lower bound on Brier skill above zero — the best is S7 at a -0.6490% lower bound (point estimate -0.2662%). After adjusting for 10 tests in the family, every q-value is 1.000. The honest headline is therefore: no strategy on this site currently shows separable skill, and a leaderboard that ranked these rules by return would be ranking noise.

Why that is the useful result. The controls bound the noise from below: a coin flip on the closing spread returns -2.4706% per bet (vig 2.70% of the handle), so a strategy that does not beat that has not beaten the cost of playing. 11 of 11 strategies have a per-bet ROI whose game-cluster lower bound sits below zero, which is what "indistinguishable from a lucky run" looks like when it is measured instead of asserted.

What would change the answer. A prospective sample in the escrow ledger, not another cut of the backtest. The evidence that would support or refute each rule is already frozen in the pre-registration ledger, so the answer cannot be fitted to the question afterwards.
